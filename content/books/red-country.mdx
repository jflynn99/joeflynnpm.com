A welcome return to the scene for some of the heaviest hitters in the series - advanced another 10 years from our last outing. For the main man "Lamb", this is a long time after we last saw him and he starts out a different person. The newer characters become both more interesting and more rounded. Shy is great, as is the gifted yet cowardly Temple - he is the highlight.

This is First Law western style. We have "Ghosts" in place of Native Americans, and a real Wild West style frontier being encroached by prospectors and modernity. Lots of this is familiar from westerns of course, but really quite different for the First Law world, and despite it being one of my favourite historical periods, I don't feel it all lands.

Lamb, a nine fingered northman, is trying his best to renounce violence, to the point that he is a total walkover. Until he's not. It is fun to see him grow back to his old self, but once almost there, the plot armour becomes too much, and the ending here for him makes no sense - the door was open, he had the space to be happy. Feels weird. I'd say let him go forever or let him be happy. The book feels too long as well. There is a long journey and then another quest - maybe both could be shorter. A huge mystery with the Dragon people is simply left hanging. Did we need such detail in Crease? No.

This said, it remains a rollicking adventure that I thoroughly enjoyed! The ending outside of Lamb is really good. All parts with Cosca are fantastic as you would expect.
