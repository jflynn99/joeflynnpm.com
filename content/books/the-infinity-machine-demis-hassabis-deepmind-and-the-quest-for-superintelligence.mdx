An outstanding story, biography, and history of the last twenty years or so in AI. It will make my (and many others') book of the year lists without doubt.

Mallaby frames Demis Hassabis as a scientist concerned with the big questions of the universe rather than the mundanity of racing to build and manage a billion-user product, and I think it is a fair portrait. It is also the story of an incredibly talented and determined person, one who has been crystal clear about what he wants to do - create artificial general intelligence - for decades.

The journey is the best part: the chess prodigy's epiphany at twelve, Bullfrog and the Theme Park games, the £500k cheque he turned down at seventeen, the failed games company, the neuroscience PhD, and then DeepMind itself. Move 37, AlphaGo Zero, and protein folding are all handled with real clarity, as is the invention of the transformer at Google and the lead they failed to press home.

To its credit the book does not shy away from the big questions. There is Spinoza and Gödel alongside the Nobel Prize, a particle collider in space, and a genuine reckoning with what happens if this all goes wrong. Demis never intended a capitalistic race between labs, rather a quiet scientific endeavour, and watching that plan meet reality is the quiet tragedy running underneath.
