This is a massive, sprawling work of great thought and depth - literally. It is my first of Neal Stephenson's - won't be my last.

It is legendary in the tech-adjacent space. After seeing so many recommendations I finally bit the bullet (it's ~1,000 pages). I knew almost nothing coming in and was surprised to find out a good half of it is a WW2 book! Home turf, love it.

The story jumps between two main time periods, WW2 and the turn of the century, as the offspring of the original cast come together to close the open threads. The cast, a confusing blend of real and fictional people, is huge, and the real history is just as freely mixed with invention.

The action is fantastic, globetrotting, immersive, brutal. The pace is mostly frenetic, there's a lot of it - but be warned there are tangents. The WW2 thread has the war of course - the usual theatres but also little-known ones like the Swedish/Finnish border and the cannibal-infested jungles of New Guinea. Then there's plenty of mathematics and cryptology. I really learned a lot, hey with the likes of Alan Turing explaining it would be hard not to! Some of the maths stories are delightful, as is the jaunty language - the book is very funny. There are some great burns on what would later be called "woke", especially with one snooty academic.

The main reasons the book remains popular, the thoughts on technology, the internet, cryptography, and what we now call crypto (digital currencies), are indeed prescient and remain deeply interesting. There are lots of arcane details. I did not expect to come out of this with so much mining knowledge but here I am.

In several other huge books I have noticed it is hard to end them well. The mystery and the drama are built up for so long it is easy to disappoint. The ending here is fine, it did not blow me away but I would not call it bad. Maybe it needed to be this way. It could have been a lot shorter, the WW2 story becomes quite fantastic and the modern day one a bit flat with all the furniture talk. I saw another reviewer call it a modern day "Moby Dick" and it is quite apt! Man I loathe that book, it is for sure better than that, and I did say it goes deep - we have submarines, diving, undersea cables, and a lot of mines. Few books take you this far down.
